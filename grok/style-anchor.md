# Style anchor: how the table's scenes read

These passages set the voice of every scene: second person, present tense, full sentences carrying several senses, bodies placed in space, people speaking in their own voices. They are here for their rhythm and density, not their content; none of these places or people exist in any campaign. Never reuse their details, and never copy a sentence.

The worked examples in `skill-dnd-narration.md` (a search with a recall, and a chase with a missed throw) belong with these. Read both files before writing.

## A quiet stretch of road

*No roll. The player said they walk on toward the town, taking their time.*

The road bends away from the river and climbs through a stand of alders, and for a while the only sounds are your own boots in the packed dirt and the water talking to itself somewhere below. The morning has gone warm without your noticing; sweat has gathered under the pack straps and cooled there, and every time you shift the weight a fresh line of it runs down between your shoulder blades. Midges hang in the shafts of light between the trees. You walk through a cloud of them and come out the other side blinking, spitting, one of them caught in the corner of your eye.

Past the alders the land opens into pasture, hummocky and bright, with sheep scattered across it like dropped washing. A dry-stone wall runs beside the road, patched in places with newer stone that hasn't yet gone grey with lichen, and somebody has wedged a horseshoe into a gap in it, points up, rusted to the colour of old blood. Far ahead, where the valley narrows, a thread of smoke goes straight up into the still air and then leans all at once to the east, as if it had changed its mind.

By the time the sun is fully behind you, your calves have settled into the long ache of a day's walking, the kind that stops being pain and becomes a sort of rhythm. The smoke is closer now. Under it you can make out roofs, a cluster of them, and the slow turning of something large and wooden against the sky.

## A conversation that goes badly

*The player asked the ferryman, bluntly, who paid him to keep quiet. The persuasion roll failed.*

The ferryman doesn't answer at once. He finishes coiling the rope, loop over loop over his broad forearm, and hangs it on its peg before he turns to look at you, and when he does it's with the flat, patient expression of a man who has been asked worse questions by people with bigger knives. His hands are enormous and cracked across every knuckle, and he wipes them slowly on a rag that makes them no cleaner.

"Paid," he says, as if tasting the word. "You hear that, girl?" This is to the child sitting on an upturned bucket at the end of the jetty, dangling a line into the brown water; she doesn't look up. "Stranger thinks I'm bought." He steps closer, near enough that you smell the river on him, weed and wet wool and the sourness of yesterday's ale, and his voice drops to something almost friendly. "I'll tell you who pays me. Whoever wants to be on the other side. A copper a head, two for a horse. That's the whole of my business, and it's none of yours."

Behind him the child has stopped pretending to fish. She is watching you now, sharp-eyed under a fringe of hair cut with a knife, and when the ferryman turns back to the boat she lifts one hand from her line and points, very briefly, at the far bank, to the place where the willows come down to the water and a second, smaller boat lies hidden in their shade.

"Crossing or not?" the ferryman says, without looking round. "Tide won't wait on your questions."

## A rest that doesn't last

*The party settled in for a long rest at the inn. In the second hour, an encounter interrupts it.*

The room is small and smells of old smoke and the lavender someone has stuffed into the pillow ticking to hide the damp. You take the bed by the wall; your companion takes the floor by the door without being asked, boots off and sword in reach, the way soldiers sleep. For a while you lie listening to the inn settle around you: a burst of laughter from the taproom, a door, feet on the stairs, the long creak of the sign outside swinging in a wind that has come up since supper. The lavender is too sweet. You turn the pillow over and the damp side is cool against your cheek, and somewhere in the middle of deciding whether that is better, you sleep.

You wake without knowing why. The room is black, the fire down to a red eye in the grate, and the wind has dropped; the sign outside hangs silent. Then you hear it again, the sound that woke you: a slow, dragging scrape, wood on wood, from below the window. Your companion is already up on one knee by the door, a shape against the faint grey of the shutter, head tilted, one hand raised flat to keep you still. The scrape stops. In the silence after it, very clearly, something taps twice on the outside of the shutter, as politely as a guest.

## The first moments of a fight

*Combat begins. The player won initiative and drew their bow.*

The shutter bursts inward in a spray of splinters and cold air, and the thing that comes through it is all limbs, too many joints bending the wrong way, hauling itself over the sill with long grey fingers that leave wet prints on the wood. It smells of the river bottom. The candle stub on the table gutters and nearly dies, and in the lurching light you see its face turn toward the bed, flat and wide-mouthed and patient, the eyes two pale discs that catch the ember-glow like a cat's.

You're faster than it is. The bow is in your hands before you have finished standing, the string cold against your fingers, an arrow nocked by touch alone; you've done this in the dark a hundred times and your body remembers even when your mind is still climbing out of sleep. Across the room your companion has the door at their back and their blade out, low and steady, the steel a dull line in the gloom. The creature drops from the sill to the floorboards with a sound like a wet sack, and the boards groan under a weight far greater than its thin body should carry. It hasn't looked at the door at all. It is looking at you.
